# MMEL and MEL concepts

## MMEL versus MEL

The **Master Minimum Equipment List (MMEL)** is the FAA-approved document for an
aircraft type. It lists equipment that may be inoperative while the aircraft is
dispatched, together with the conditions and the repair interval. The
**Minimum Equipment List (MEL)** is the operator's own document, derived from
the MMEL and approved for that operator's fleet and procedures. An operator MEL
may be more restrictive than the MMEL; it may not be less restrictive. When a
crew chief reports a defect, the ground agent looks up the operator's MEL item,
not just the MMEL page.

## Repair categories (PL-25)

An MMEL/MEL item has a repair category that sets how long the item may remain
inoperative:

- **Category A** — the interval is stated in the item's remarks. There is no
  generic number; read the remarks.
- **Category B** — repair within **3 consecutive calendar days**, excluding the
  day of discovery.
- **Category C** — repair within **10 consecutive calendar days**, excluding the
  day of discovery.
- **Category D** — repair within **120 consecutive calendar days**, excluding the
  day of discovery.

"Excluding the day of discovery" means the clock starts on the day after the
defect is found. If an item has no relief in the MEL, it is **not deferrable**;
the aircraft cannot be dispatched until it is repaired, regardless of the
category letters used elsewhere in the document.

## (M) and (O) procedures

**(M)** means a maintenance procedure is required before dispatch: for example,
deactivate a system, secure a valve closed, placard a switch, or perform a
specific inspection. **(O)** means an operational procedure is required: for
example, limit flight altitude, prohibit extended overwater flight, or require
the crew to use an alternate procedure. **(M)(O)** means both apply. A
maintenance action without the required operational procedure (or the reverse)
does not satisfy the MEL.

## Deferral process

The normal sequence is: record the discrepancy, identify the MEL item, confirm
the repair category and any remarks, carry out the (M) action, brief the crew on
the (O) action, placard or deactivate as required, enter the deferral in the
aircraft technical log, and schedule the repair before the category interval
expires. If the item is not deferrable, or the conditions cannot be met, the
aircraft stays on the ground until rectification. The MEL does not authorize
dispatching with a known unsafe condition; it only permits specific, approved
inoperative equipment under stated conditions.
