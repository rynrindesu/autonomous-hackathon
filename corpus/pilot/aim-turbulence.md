# Turbulence PIREPs and intensity definitions

## What a turbulence PIREP should state

AIM §7-1-21 asks pilots who encounter turbulence to report it to ATC as soon as
practicable. A turbulence PIREP should state the aircraft location, time of
occurrence in UTC, turbulence intensity, whether the turbulence was in or near
clouds, aircraft altitude or flight level, aircraft type and duration. Two AIM
examples are: "Over Omaha, 1232Z, moderate turbulence in clouds at Flight Level
three one zero, Boeing 707" and "From five zero miles south of Albuquerque to
three zero miles north of Phoenix, 1250Z, occasional moderate chop at Flight
Level three three zero, DC8."

Duration and classification should use the AIM turbulence reporting criteria
table (Table 7-1-11 in the plan's citation; the current AIM HTML labels the
criteria table TBL 7-1-21). The table separates the aircraft reaction, the
reaction inside the aircraft and the reporting term.

## Intensity definitions

**Light** turbulence momentarily causes slight, erratic changes in altitude
and/or attitude (pitch, roll, yaw), or slight, rapid and somewhat rhythmic
bumpiness without appreciable altitude or attitude change (light chop).
Occupants may feel slight strain against belts; food service can continue.

**Moderate** turbulence is similar to light but more intense. Altitude and/or
attitude changes occur, but the aircraft remains in positive control at all
times, and indicated airspeed usually varies. Occupants feel definite strain
against belts, unsecured objects are dislodged, and food service and walking
are difficult. "Moderate chop" is rapid bumps or jolts without appreciable
altitude or attitude change.

**Severe** turbulence causes large, abrupt changes in altitude and/or attitude
and usually large variations in indicated airspeed. The aircraft may be
momentarily out of control. Occupants are forced violently against belts,
unsecured objects are tossed about, and food service and walking are
impossible.

**Extreme** turbulence tosses the aircraft violently and makes it practically
impossible to control; it may cause structural damage.

## Frequency, chop and clear-air turbulence

Intensity is also qualified by frequency: occasional (less than one third of
the time), intermittent (one third to two thirds) and continuous (more than two
thirds). High-level turbulence above about 15,000 ft not associated with
cumuliform cloud should be reported as clear-air turbulence (CAT) with the
appropriate intensity, for example "light CAT" or "moderate chop". For this
project, moderate turbulence is a soft penalty and severe turbulence at a cruise
level is a hard no-go.
