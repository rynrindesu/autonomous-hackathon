# 14 CFR 121.639 fuel supply

## Domestic fuel requirement

For all domestic operations, §121.639 says that no person may dispatch or take
off an airplane unless it has enough fuel:

- (a) to fly to the airport to which it is dispatched;
- (b) thereafter, to fly to and land at the most distant alternate airport
  (where required) for the airport to which dispatched; and
- (c) thereafter, to fly for **45 minutes at normal cruising fuel
  consumption** (or 30 minutes for certain day-VFR nontransport category
  airplanes type certificated after December 31, 1964, under an operations
  specification authorization).

The rule is written as a sequence: destination, then the most distant required
alternate, then the 45-minute reserve. "Where required" matters: if no alternate
is required for the flight, the alternate leg is zero, but the 45-minute
reserve still applies. The reserve is measured at normal cruising fuel
consumption, not at holding burn.

## Applying the rule to a landing decision

This project does not use the §121.639 figure for its hard check. The
organisers' constraints workbook (challenge-2 workbook: fuel_and_crew) sets the
final reserve at **30 minutes of holding fuel** (never plan below it), with
contingency of 5% of trip fuel and an assumed spare of 45 minutes of holding on
top of the final reserve. PLT-FUEL-01 compares the planned landing fuel with
that final reserve; PLT-FUEL-02 allows only as many holding minutes as the fuel
above the final reserve supports. §121.639 is background on the real domestic
rule.

Do not confuse §121.639 with §121.645. Section 121.639 is the domestic rule
with a 45-minute normal-cruising reserve. Section 121.645 applies to flag and
supplemental operations and uses a 30-minute holding reserve at 1,500 ft above
the alternate. The two numbers are not interchangeable.
