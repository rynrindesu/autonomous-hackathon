# 14 CFR 121.645 flag and supplemental fuel

## The 30-minute holding reserve

§121.645 applies to turbine-engine powered airplanes (other than turboprops)
in flag and supplemental operations. For operations outside the 48 contiguous
United States and the District of Columbia, the rule requires enough fuel,
considering wind and expected weather, to fly to the destination, then to the
most distant alternate specified in the flight release if an alternate is
required, and after that to fly for **30 minutes at holding speed at 1,500 feet
above the alternate airport** (or above the destination if no alternate is
required), under standard temperature conditions.

This is the rule that is often quoted as "30 minutes at 1,500 feet". It is not
the domestic §121.639 rule. In §121.639 the reserve is 45 minutes at normal
cruising fuel consumption after the most distant required alternate. Do not
apply the flag/supplemental 30-minute holding figure to a domestic flight, and
do not describe it as a 45-minute reserve.

## Why the distinction matters

The two reserves are measured differently (normal cruise versus holding at
1,500 ft) and belong to different operation types. An agent that mixes them can
understate or overstate the fuel needed for a delay. In this project, the
pilot's hard fuel check uses the domestic framing (alternate plus 45 minutes at
normal cruise). If a scenario is labelled as a flag or supplemental operation,
the policy should be changed explicitly and the citation switched to §121.645.

The section also contains a separate two-hour rule for release to an airport
for which no alternate is specified under certain conditions. That rule is not
the 30-minute holding reserve and should not be substituted for it.
