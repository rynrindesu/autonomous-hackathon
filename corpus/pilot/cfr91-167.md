# 14 CFR 91.167 IFR fuel requirements

## IFR fuel rule

§91.167 states that no person may operate a civil aircraft in IFR conditions
unless it carries enough fuel, considering weather reports, forecasts and
weather conditions, to:

1. complete the flight to the first airport of intended landing;
2. except as provided in paragraph (b), fly from that airport to the alternate
   airport; and
3. fly after that for **45 minutes at normal cruising speed** or, for
   helicopters, **30 minutes at normal cruising speed**.

Paragraph (b) removes the alternate-airport leg when an instrument approach
procedure is available at the destination and the weather permits a descent
from the minimum en-route altitude to the approach without relying on other
than standard instruments and navigation aids. Even then, the 45-minute
(airplane) or 30-minute (helicopter) reserve after the intended landing remains.

## Practical reading for an airline flight

The 45-minute figure is a **normal-cruising** reserve, not a holding-time
allowance. It is the same order of magnitude as the domestic airline rule in
§121.639, but the two rules apply to different operations and should be cited
separately. In this project, PLT-FUEL-01 uses the organisers' workbook
instead (challenge-2 workbook: fuel_and_crew): never plan below a final reserve
of 30 minutes of holding fuel. §91.167 is the general IFR backstop and explains
why a final reserve is treated as a hard minimum rather than a discretionary
buffer.

The reserve does not authorize the crew to plan to land with less fuel. It
defines the fuel that must remain at the planned landing point. If a delay
would eat into that reserve, the correct response is to reduce delay, change
the destination or alternate, or declare minimum fuel — not to continue and
hope the weather improves.
